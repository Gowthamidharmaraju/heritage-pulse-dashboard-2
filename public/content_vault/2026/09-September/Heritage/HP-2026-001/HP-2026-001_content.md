# Traditional Temple Architecture of Andhra Pradesh: The Dravidian & Chalukyan Marvels

**Category**: Heritage | **Status**: PUBLISHED
**Writer**: Pavitra


                
                <h2 rtrvr-ls="5~hs,6~hs,8~hs,9~hs" rtrvr-ro="42">The Majesty of Deccan Stone</h2><p rtrvr-ls="5~hs,6~hs,8~hs,9~hs,49~hs" rtrvr-ro="42">The temple architecture of Andhra Pradesh represents a magnificent confluence of <strong rtrvr-ls="5~hs,6~hs,8~hs,9~hs" rtrvr-ro="42">Dravidian</strong>, <em rtrvr-ls="5~hs,6~hs,8~hs,9~hs" rtrvr-ro="42">Chalukyan</em>, and Vijayanagara idioms. Standing before the monolithic Nandi at Lepakshi or gazing upon the floating pillar of Veerabhadra Temple, one is struck by the peerless mastery of medieval Indian stone masons.</p><h3 rtrvr-ls="5~hs,6~hs,8~hs,9~hs" rtrvr-ro="42">1. The Hanging Pillar of Lepakshi</h3><p rtrvr-ls="5~hs,6~hs,8~hs,9~hs" rtrvr-ro="42">Built in the 16th century by the brothers Virupanna and Veeranna, the Veerabhadra temple in Lepakshi is renowned for its 70 stone pillars. Among them, one legendary pillar does not touch the ground completely, allowing thin cloths to pass underneath—a testament to precise load balancing and stone joinery.</p><h3 rtrvr-ls="49~hs" rtrvr-ro="42">2. Srisailam: The Hilltop Citadel</h3><p>Perched on the Nallamala hills, the Bhramaramba Mallikarjuna Temple showcases massive stone prakarams (outer walls) carved with military pageantry, mythological friezes, and pastoral life scenes.</p><blockquote rtrvr-ls="49~hs" rtrvr-ro="42">"The temple was not merely a place of worship; it was an epicenter of art, dance, community granary, and astronomical observation."</blockquote>
